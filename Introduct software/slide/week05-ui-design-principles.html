▶ intro2se-w5-ui.fig · UX vs UI
TWO WORDS, OFTEN CONFUSED

UX = does it work. UI = does it look and feel right.

UX · User Experience

The flow & the logic

Can the user reach their goal quickly, without confusion? Steps, structure, error states, "where am I / what next." Mostly invisible when it's good.

UI · User Interface

The look & the surface

Type, color, spacing, layout, the exact button. The part people see — and judge in the first half-second.

Today leans UI: the visible craft. But good UI serves the UX — pretty that hides the next step is bad design, not good design.

▶ intro2se-w5-ui.fig · Principle · C.R.A.P
PRINCIPLE 2 · FOUR LETTERS THAT FIX MOST LAYOUTS

C.R.A.P — the checklist designers actually use

C

Contrast

Make different things look clearly different — big vs small, dark vs light.

R

Repetition

Reuse the same styles (your tokens) so it feels like one product.

A

Alignment

Put things on a grid — every edge lines up, nothing floats.

P

Proximity

Group related things close; push unrelated things apart.

Robin Williams' classic framework. When a layout feels "off" but you can't say why, it's almost always one of these four.

Pick gaps from one scale and let it breathe. Beginners cram; pros add room — same content, very different feel.

More gap between groups, less within a group (that's Proximity again). Spacing is the cheapest upgrade in all of UI.

▶ intro2se-w5-ui.fig · Principle · Color
PRINCIPLE 5 · LESS COLOR, USED ON PURPOSE

Color: a small palette, one accent, enough contrast

60 / 30 / 10 — mostly neutral, one bold accent for actions only. Semantic (green=ok, red=danger). Text contrast ≥ 4.5:1 (WCAG AA).

✗ RAINBOW · low contrast
Grey text on grey — hard to read (2.0:1 ✗)
✓ NEUTRAL + 1 ACCENT · 60/30/10
Dark text on white — easy AA ✓ 12:1

Rainbow UIs look amateur. A restrained palette with one confident accent looks like a product someone designed.

▶ intro2se-w5-ui.fig · Principle · Nielsen's 10
PRINCIPLE 6 · THE UX CHECKLIST · JAKOB NIELSEN (1994)

10 usability heuristics — review any screen against these

1 · Visibility of status — show what's happening progress bar

2 · Match real world — familiar words & icons calendar app

3 · User control & freedom — undo, cancel, exit Dropbox

4 · Consistency & standards — platform conventions Spotify

5 · Error prevention — block mistakes early Airbnb dates

6 · Recognition over recall — show, don't force recall Nike menu

7 · Flexibility & efficiency — shortcuts for pros Figma

8 · Aesthetic & minimalist — cut what doesn't help Apple

9 · Recover from errors — plain errors + a fix Lemonade

10 · Help & documentation — help when needed ChatGPT

A heuristic evaluation: walk any prototype against these 10 and note each violation. A great lens for reviewing another team's build.

▶ intro2se-w5-ui.fig · Principle · Accessibility
PRINCIPLE 7 · DESIGN FOR EVERYONE · a11y / WCAG

Accessibility: POUR — and it's just good UX too

P

Perceivable

Text contrast ≥ 4.5:1, alt text on images, never rely on color alone.

O

Operable

Everything works by keyboard, a visible focus state, targets ~44px.

U

Understandable

Clear language, labelled forms, errors that say how to fix.

R

Robust

Semantic HTML (<button> <nav> <label>) so screen readers work.

Aim for WCAG AA (A = minimum · AA = the standard · AAA = highest). Accessible design helps everyone — and in many places it's the law.

▶ intro2se-w5-ui.fig · The AI look
THE PROBLEM YOU'VE ALL NOTICED

Why does every AI-generated UI look the same?

The reason

AI outputs the average

A model predicts the most likely next pixel or line of markup. With a vague prompt, "most likely" = the average of millions of look-alike SaaS landing pages in its training data. You get the statistical median of the internet.

The consequence

Regression to the mean

Nothing pulls it off that average unless you do. No point of view in → no point of view out. "Make me a nice landing page" always lands in the same place.

This isn't a bug. It's what "predict the most probable output" means. The fix is to make your desired output less average — on purpose.

▶ intro2se-w5-ui.fig · Spot the AI look
THE "AI SLOP" CHECKLIST · YOU'LL SEE THESE EVERYWHERE NOW

Tell-tale signs of an un-directed AI UI

🟣 Purple-to-blue gradient everywhere (hero, buttons, blobs)

🅰️ Default system / Inter font, no real type choice

🎯 Everything centered, one column, huge hero headline

🟦 Rounded-2xl cards + glassmorphism + soft shadows on all

🚀 Emoji as icons (🚀✨💡) instead of a real icon set

📝 Lorem-ipsum / generic copy ("Supercharge your workflow")

⚖️ No clear hierarchy — everything the same weight

🧩 Feature-card grid of 3, identical, with tiny icons

None of these are wrong on their own — they're just the default. A whole screen made only of defaults is the tell.

▶ intro2se-w5-ui.fig · Pro move 1
HOW TO LOOK PROFESSIONAL · 1 OF 3

Have a point of view — steal from real products, not the default

AVERAGE IN → AVERAGE OUT

"Make it modern and clean"

Vague words let the AI fall back to its median aesthetic. You've described nothing.

GIVE IT A REFERENCE

Pick 1–2 real apps you admire

Notion's calm typography, Linear's tight spacing, your bank's data density. Screenshot them → that's your reference image + spec.

A specific spec + a real reference image (from the Designing with Claude deck) is exactly what pulls the output off the mean. Taste = choosing what to copy.

▶ intro2se-w5-ui.fig · Pro move 3
HOW TO LOOK PROFESSIONAL · 3 OF 3

Prompt the AI away from its defaults

Refine this UI to look professional, not AI-generated: - No purple/blue gradient, no glassmorphism, no emoji icons. - Typeface: "Source Serif" headings + "Inter" body (one clear pairing). - Palette: my brand neutrals + one accent (hex below), 60/30/10. - Strong hierarchy: one focal element per screen. - Left-align text; use real copy, not lorem. - Match the reference image for density and tone. - Add hover / focus / empty states. Then list what still looks generic so I can push further.

Every "no ___" removes one default. Every "use ___" adds a decision. That's the whole trick: replace the AI's averages with your choices.
