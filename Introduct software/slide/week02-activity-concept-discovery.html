▶ intro2se-concept-discovery.fig · Step 1
FORM THE TEAM 15 MIN

Create a team with complementary strengths

Facilitator

Guide the process

Protect time and make sure every voice is heard.

Researcher

Seek evidence

Interview users and preserve their exact words.

Recorder

Make thinking visible

Capture pain points, clusters, and decisions.

Challenger / Presenter

Test assumptions

Ask “How do we know?” and communicate the result.

Team size: 4–5 students. Rotate roles during future activities.

▶ intro2se-concept-discovery.fig · Step 2
PAIN-POINT BRAIN DUMP 10 MIN

Start with problems—not app ideas

Write 3–5 pain points each

Work silently first. Use situations you have experienced or directly observed.

Learning and time management
Campus services and administration
Teamwork, health, travel, or community
Local organizations, shops, or public services

Do not write: “Build an AI scheduling app.”   Write: who struggles, when, and with what consequence.

▶ intro2se-concept-discovery.fig · Pain-point format
MAKE THE OBSERVATION SPECIFIC

Use a four-part pain-point statement

User
who?
+
Difficulty
what is hard?
+
Context
when and where?
+
Impact
what happens?

Students and staff struggle to obtain reliable university-policy answers because rules are scattered across long, changing documents, causing delays and decisions based on incomplete information.

Possible future challenge: a citation-grounded RAG system—but only if the evidence supports this problem.

▶ intro2se-concept-discovery.fig · Step 3
EVIDENCE HUNT 20 MIN

Investigate before you believe

Select

2–3 candidate problems

Choose the most promising pain points for rapid investigation.

Interview

At least two people

Speak with users outside your team about recent real events.

Capture

Evidence, not opinions

Quotes, frequency, impact, and current workaround.

Ask for the most recent example. Avoid “Would you use our app?”

▶ intro2se-concept-discovery.fig · Interview guide
ASK ABOUT BEHAVIOR, NOT FUTURE PROMISES

Five interview questions

01Tell me about the last time this problem happened.
02What were you trying to accomplish?
03Which part was most difficult, slow, or frustrating?
04How do you handle the problem today?
05What happens if the problem is not solved?
▶ intro2se-concept-discovery.fig · AI boundary
AI-FIRST DOES NOT MEAN EVIDENCE-FREE

Use AI after collecting real evidence

AI MAY HELP

Organize and summarize

Cluster notes, identify contradictions, and draft clearer wording.

Challenge assumptions

Suggest missing stakeholders, risks, and interview questions.

AI MAY NOT REPLACE

Real users

Do not generate fake interviews or invented evidence.

Team accountability

Verify every summary against the original notes.

Find patterns across the evidence

Raw notes
quotes + events
→
Affinity clusters
similar pains
→
Candidate problems
clear patterns

evidence + scope

Merge similar observations. Keep conflicting evidence visible instead of hiding it.

▶ intro2se-concept-discovery.fig · Prioritization
CHOOSE THE PROBLEM, NOT THE COOLEST TECHNOLOGY

Six selection criteria

A specific user group experiences it
Evidence comes from real events
The problem occurs frequently enough
The impact is meaningful
Current workarounds are inadequate
The team can investigate it this semester

Decision rule: select one focus and record why the other candidates were not selected.

▶ intro2se-concept-discovery.fig · Step 5
FRAME THE PROBLEM 20 MIN

Write the problem before the product

Problem statement template

[User group] needs a way to [achieve a need] because [current difficulty], resulting in [impact].

Students and staff need a way to locate current, verifiable university-policy information because relevant rules are distributed across long documents and websites, resulting in slow answers and decisions based on outdated or unsupported information.

▶ intro2se-concept-discovery.fig · Step 6
EXPLORE CONCEPTS 15 MIN

Generate alternatives before choosing

Concept A

Curated FAQ process

Policy owners maintain verified answers and update dates manually.

Concept B

Structured policy search

Search, filters, version labels, and direct links to official sections.

Concept C

Secure citation RAG

Grounded answers, abstention, source citations, and prompt-injection tests.

RAG, embeddings, and model selection belong to later design decisions—not the initial problem statement.

▶ intro2se-concept-discovery.fig · Step 7
60-SECOND PITCH 15 MIN

Present evidence before solution

Problem

Who + what + impact

State the focused problem in one sentence.

Evidence

Why believe it?

Use events, quotes, frequency, and workarounds.

Concept

What might help?

Show alternatives and the current preferred direction.

PROBLEM–OPPORTUNITY BRIEF

The Concept-stage deliverable

Team + users

Roles and the specific stakeholder group.

Problem

Need + impact

Real events

▶ intro2se-concept-discovery.fig · Next SDLC stage
ONLY AFTER THE CONCEPT GATE

The project enters Development

01 · Requirements

Define behavior and quality

Citations, abstention, privacy, latency, and security criteria.

02 · Architecture

Design components and trust

Documents, retrieval, model, guardrails, and access boundaries.

03 · Implementation

Build the selected concept

Search or RAG pipeline, model integration, and controls.

04 · V&V + Deployment

Produce evidence and release

Retrieval quality, hallucination, injection resistance, and rollout.

Key idea: the SDLC starts by confirming the right problem for the right stakeholders—not when coding begins.
