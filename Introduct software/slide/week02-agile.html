WEEK 2 · BASED ON SOMMERVILLE, CH. 3

Agile Software Development

Balancing discipline and flexibility in modern engineering — from rigid planning to agile adaptability. Emphasises individuals & customer collaboration, working software over documentation, and responding to change over following a plan.

▶ intro2se-w2-agile.fig · Why Agile
THE DRIVER

The challenge of rapid change

The Driver

Rapid development and delivery are now the critical requirements for software systems.

The Problem

Business environments change faster than comprehensive software specifications can be written.

The Trade-off

Organizations often trade software quality or complete requirements for faster time-to-market.

"By the time the software is available for use, the original reason for its procurement may have changed so radically that the software is effectively useless."

— Ian Sommerville
▶ intro2se-w2-agile.fig · Two approaches
PROCESS

Plan-driven vs Agile development

Plan-driven

Separate, sequential stages

Requirements → design → implementation → testing. The output of one stage is the input to the next, and the process relies on explicit documentation.

requirements→design→build→test
Agile

Interleaved activities

Specification and design happen during implementation. Agile relies on implicit knowledge and team communication rather than heavy documents.

spec↺design↺build

▶ intro2se-w2-agile.fig · Fit
DECISION

When to use Agile?

The sweet spot

Product: small to medium-sized custom systems.
Team: small, co-located teams with informal communication.
Client: customers willing to be continuously involved.

When plan-driven is better

Scale: large, complex systems requiring pre-analysis.
Criticality: safety-critical systems needing safety cases (certification).
Geography: distributed teams with infrequent communication.

▶ intro2se-w2-agile.fig · XP practices
XP PRACTICES

Keeping code clean

Practice 01

Incremental Planning

Requirements are recorded on story cards. Releases are very small and frequent.

Practice 02

Simple Design

Design only for current requirements. Designing for future change is often wasted effort.

Practice 03

Refactoring

Developers refactor continuously. Code is kept simple and maintainable as improvements are found.

▶ intro2se-w2-agile.fig · XP practices
XP PRACTICES

Collaboration and ownership

People

Pair Programming

Two programmers work at one workstation. One writes code, the other reviews it immediately — diffusing knowledge across the team.

Code

Collective Ownership

No single person "owns" a module. Any pair can improve any part of the code at any time.

Pace

Sustainable Pace

Large amounts of overtime are unacceptable. A rested team is a reliable team.

LIGHTWEIGHT REQUIREMENTS

Requirements as user stories

The Concept

Replace heavy documentation with "story cards" and scenarios.

The Conversation

The card is a placeholder for a discussion between developer and customer.

The Task

The team breaks these stories down into implementation tasks.

Story card · example

Prescribing Medication

Kate is a doctor who wishes to prescribe medication for a patient. She selects "current medication" or "new medication" from the patient record. The system checks the dose against the approved range and asks her to confirm.

▶ intro2se-w2-agile.fig · Scrum
MANAGING THE PROCESS, NOT THE CODE

The Scrum management framework

Scrum facilitates a psychological shift from "command and control" to "self-organizing teams".

✗ Command & Control

Top-down hierarchy

A PM directs middle management, who direct the workers. Decisions flow one way — down.

✓ Self-Organizing Team

The team decides how

The team makes decisions on execution together. It decides how to do the work, not just what.

▶ intro2se-w2-agile.fig · Limits
THE REALITY

Scaling Agile has limits

⚠ Limit 01

System of Systems

Large systems are often collections of standalone systems. Agile struggles with cross-system negotiation.

⚠ Limit 02

External Regulations

Safety-critical systems (e.g. FAA certification) require extensive documentation Agile often deprioritizes.

⚠ Limit 03

Procurement

Fixed-price contracts conflict with Agile's flexible scope.

▶ intro2se-w2-agile.fig · Evolution
THE TRADE-OFF

Maintenance & evolution risks

The Risk

Agile relies on team continuity. If the team breaks up, knowledge is lost.

The Reality

Traditional documentation is often outdated anyway.

The Solution

Automated test suites act as living documentation for new developers.

Implicit knowledge

The team knows the code

Documentation

Manuals are sparse
